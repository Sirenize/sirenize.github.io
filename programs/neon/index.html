i never noticed the leaves before

their slight hint of brown

the way they blow and fall in the wind

it’s peaceful.

it’s quiet.

walking through the city, i see you

in a sea of faces, plastered upon banners hanging high

people walk by, they talk about them

they talk about you.

they praise it.

they accept it.

they understand it,

but never resent it.

so focused on the skies,

so loud and colorful.

they forget to look around,

towards the trees, towards each other.

they’d notice the leaves

their slight hint of brown

the way they blow and fall in the wind

they’d see it’s peaceful.

it’s quiet.

Comments:

[a]this could mean that when a person is in a dark place, they believe that the only option is suicide. so telling the audience or the listener to keep their wits that will hold them back from that solution

[b]the narrator doesnt finish the sentence, "when they..."

this could imply that they have had experience with losing a loved one to suicide or even struggled with it themself

[c]"treat a loss like it's a win" emphasizes that killing yourself could gain you more of a reputation

[d]this is communicating the overall message of the song that it is not suicide that "our culture" should praise, not that the person should not be remembered, but that the culture should praise the people who survived and lived past the dark nights to an older age

[e]"try to call" implies that it is calling directly to the narrator, the idea of the "neon gravestone"

[f]the title, "neon gravestones" is an oxymoron describing how bright and alluding the idea may seem with how we shouldnt be glorifying it as a solution.

[g]here, the narrator could be explaining that it is not that suicide should not be mourned and remembered, but it should not be treated as a beacon to those who are struggling, emphasizing the option of suicide over reaching out. but instead to not "get it twisted"

[h]the narrator speaks about how they are also struggling with these thoughts, saying that it is an option they have considered and they know that it would give them the easy fame

[i]"I'll mourn for a kid, but won't cry for a king" implies that the narrator will not mourn the loss of the creator and the fame that they receive but they will mourn the loss of life, using "kid" to emphasize the youth and innocence of the individual.

[j]the narrator is asking not to be praised like others have been if he too falls to suicide, saying to simply move on to someone else instead of mourning, even for a little.

[k]the narrator is describing that despite this "rise in awareness" for suicide and depression, which is helping prevent it, could also be seen "from a new point of view" where it has become more mainstream and could be seen more as an option than before due to the sudden rise in popularity/infamy

[l]the narrator uses "mistake" seemingly to describe suicide, showing their views and leading the listener to believe that it would be a mistake and not a solution

[m]the multiple forms, for lack of a better word, describe why someone might commit such an act. the narrator speaks about how it could be used as a weapon saying, "thinking 'I'll teach them,'' to describe what the person might be going through in those moments, using suicide as a means of revenge.

[n]the narrator is telling the audience that this option will/should not be heard and should be ignored when trying to find a solution to one's problems

[o]the closing lines for the song solidify the universal idea of "swapping out what we hold so high" and instead celebrating the ones who was dedicated to their life and staying true to the path
